# Engineering Log - 2026-11-22

## Make the Page Work on a Phone Held Upright

### Purpose and scope

The home page build stamp advances to 2026-11-22 with this commit.


The owner uses a phone mostly upright, with rotation locked, and on such a
phone the emulator was close to unusable. The board is drawn 300 by 100 mm
and never narrower than 760 px, so a 390 px screen showed its left half,
the regulator, CPU and ROMs, while the displays and every key were off
screen to the right behind a sideways scroll that nothing pointed to.

Three ways were put to the owner the evening before: gather the working
parts into a handheld column, turn the board on its side, or open the page
scrolled to the keypad. The owner then handed the work over for a few hours
with the instruction to decide and finish: something that works on a phone
held upright, with the desktop version left as it is. The handheld column
was chosen, because it is the only one of the three in which the keys are
large enough for a thumb and the lettering is the right way up.

**What a phone gets.** On a narrow, upright touch screen the six displays,
the four phase LEDs, the function keys with the NOR/SS switch, and the hex
keys move into one panel, styled as the board's green, at the top of the
page. Each block keeps its own arrangement from the board: the function keys
as their two rows, the hex keys as their four. The hex keys are at the
bottom, nearest the thumb. The maker's mark, as printed up the board's
right-hand edge, stands upright at the panel's foot. Under the panel the
whole board is shown at the width of the screen as a picture, with a
caption, and the bench controls follow as before.

**How.** The four parts were already HTML laid over the board's SVG, so
nothing had to be redrawn. `setupHandheldLayout()` in `main.js` moves the
live elements into the panel and leaves in the drawing an inert copy of
each, with no id, `data-key` or `title`, so that every lookup in `main.js`
still finds the live part and the copies are never given handlers. Turning
the phone moves the parts back to exactly where they were, and forward
again; the elements are the same ones throughout, so their handlers and the
references `display.js` keeps are untouched.

All of the styling is one block of rules under `html.handheld`, a class only
`main.js` sets. The parts were sized in `cqw` of the board, and inside the
panel `cqw` refers to the panel instead, so each such length is restated in
a unit `--u`, chosen so the LEDs, the shadows and the silkscreen keep their
proportion to the larger keys. The key pitch is `min(19.5cqw, 9svh)`: four
keys across, and short enough that the title, the displays and every key are
on the first screen with the browser's toolbars showing.

**Keeping the desktop as it was.** Two guards. The layout needs
`(max-width: 640px) and (orientation: portrait) and (pointer: coarse)`, and a
mouse is a fine pointer, so no desktop window, of any shape, ever gets it;
width and orientation alone would have caught a tall narrow window. And the
desktop page was screenshotted at 1400 x 1000, 1024 x 768, 800 x 1000, 700 x
1100 and 600 x 900 from the commit before the change and from the change,
with the processor stopped and reset so that both show the same state, and
compared pixel by pixel: identical at all five, the last two being portrait
windows a little narrower than the board.

**Decided and left.** A phone turned to landscape gets the board as drawn,
which then fits its width, but the title pushes the lower key rows below the
fold. The owner keeps rotation locked, and the instruction was about upright
use, so it was left and is noted in `NextSession.md`. The pictures in the
drawing are static. Tablets wider than 640 px in portrait keep the board.

- **The pictures could keep whatever was showing.** They were taken the
  first time the layout came on, so a phone that started in landscape and
  was turned upright mid-use left lit digits or a pressed key in the
  picture. They are taken at startup now, and a key's press style is
  stripped from them.

It also pointed out that a moved element lost its focus, now given back;
that the gap between the panel's blocks was measured against the screen and
not the panel, now margins on the blocks; and that Safari before 14 lacks
the newer `matchMedia` listener, now with a fallback. Its one remaining
point, that Chrome 105 to 107 has container units but not `svh`, was
accepted and is noted in `AGENTS.md`.

The browser check was extended to cover the first two: a swipe across the 7
key leaves the display as it was and the key up, a tap on it still types 7;
and after lighting two LEDs and pressing a key in landscape, the pictures
taken for the drawing show no lit segment, no lit LED and no pressed key
while the live display has 36 lit segments. A focused switch keeps its
focus through two turns. The desktop checks of HLT, single step and the
instant cassette were run again and pass, and the desktop screenshots were
compared again after the fixes: still identical at all five sizes.

## The LEDs D4-D7 Under a User's Program

### Purpose and scope

The owner asked whether the four LEDs could be used on the original machine
for something other than the cassette, and then whether the emulator could do
it. The answers were yes as far as the schematic shows, and that it already
does. So no code of the machine changed. What changed is what is written
about it, which was wrong, and the guide, which now shows how.

**What the manual says.** Only the cassette use: the assembly instructions on
supplement page 43 ("servono a sapere cosa sta accadendo quando è in funzione
il registratore") and Fig. 26 on page 45. A search of the supplement's text
found no other.

**What the schematic allows.** Page 28 has each LED on a gate of IC21 whose
inputs are Port B bit 7 and one of Port A's lines 0-3, and nothing of the
cassette circuit. Port B bit 7 does nothing else. A program that makes Port A
an output, writes a pattern to it and writes `$87` to Port B lights the LEDs
of the pattern with the displays dark: `$87` has PB1-PB4 at 3, the 74LS145
output that is not connected, and it is the value the PROM's LOAD routine
writes for the same purpose.

**What was wrong.** `AGENTS.md` (Board Artwork and Known Limitation 7), the
LEDs' tooltips on the page and a comment in `main.js` said the LEDs light
only with **Cassette: Real time**. The machine layer has never looked at the
cassette setting for them: `_phaseLEDsNow()` runs after every write to the
8255. The statement came from describing who lights them, the real routines,
as if it were a condition.

**What was added.**

- `guide.html`: a section, The four LEDs in your own program, with two
  listings at `$0200`. The first lights the pattern in the byte at `$0206`.
  The second counts in binary at about three steps a second, and sets the
  ports up again each time round so that it lights again when RUN resumes it
  after HLT.
- `tests/user-leds.test.js`: reads both listings out of the guide and runs
  them with the traps on and no recorder. All four on the whole time and no
  digit lit; each pattern the guide names; the count on the LEDs equal to
  `$0020`, one more each step, about six steps in two seconds; out after RES
  and after HLT; back after RUN.
- `AGENTS.md`: a section, The LEDs D4-D7 Under a Program, with the evidence,
  why `$87`, why the mode word comes first, and what HLT does to them.
- `README.md`, `NextSession.md`, the tooltips and the comments corrected.

### Verification

- `node --test tests/`: 198 checks pass, five of them new.
- The new test was run against a copy of `amico2000.js` changed to light the
  LEDs only with the traps off, which is what the documentation had claimed:
  four of its five checks failed, as did three older ones.
- In headless Chrome, on the page as served with **Cassette** at Instant:
  both programs were keyed in through the on-screen keypad, digit by digit.
  The first lit all four LEDs with the displays dark; RES put them out; `0A`
  at `$0206` lit D5 and D7 only; HLT put them out. The counter showed its
  count on the LEDs in 39 of 40 samples, the other being a frame in which
  the count changed, and went through all 16 patterns in 5.6 seconds; HLT
  put it out and RUN took it up again. On an emulated phone held upright the
  first program lit the four LEDs in the panel, by touch. No console errors.
- The page's tooltips and one comment in `index.html` changed and nothing
  that is drawn. The handheld layout's checks still pass.

Not verified: a real board. That the lines are free for a program is read
from the schematic and from the PROM, not observed.

## Plan for a Shelf of Programs

### Purpose and scope

No code changed. This records a decision and what was found in preparing it,
and puts the plan at the head of `NextSession.md`.

Asked at the end of the session how the project stood, the answer given was
that the board is done as far as the documents allow and that the site has
nothing for a visitor to run. The owner had come to the same view and

displays, in which a Z came out as a y. The name was a private person's and

**What it was probably built on.** The supplement has an exercise on PDF
page 68: six bytes at `$0300`, `20 7E FF 4C 00 03`, that call the monitor's
display routine at `$FF7E` in a loop, with the six bytes at `$008F-$0094`
shown one to a digit. The manual gives `77 6D 79 38 00 00` for the word ASEL
and invites the reader to enrich the program. A Z can be given the segments
of a 2. The owner's program showed a y all the same; the owner is certain of
that and does not remember why. Its G was a capital: unsure at first whether
it had been a small g drawn like a 9, the owner was shown both on the
emulated displays and recognised the capital.

## The Home Page in Italian

### Purpose and scope

The second of the three commits for the site in Italian. The guide could be a
second page; the home page could not. Its board is a thousand lines of SVG
edited by hand, and a copy for Italian would have to be kept in step with it
by hand, twice over once the shelf of programs adds its own controls. So
`index.html` stays one page and holds both languages.

How, and why this way:

- **The text is in the page twice**, an element marked `data-lang="en"` and
  straight after it the same thing marked `data-lang="it"`, and one style rule
  hides whichever is not the language of `<html lang>`. The alternative, a
  table of Italian strings swapped in by script, was not taken: the page would
  draw in English and then change, and whoever edits a sentence would not have
  its translation in front of them.
- **`lang.js` chooses, from `<head>`**, before the body is read: the language
  the address asks for (`?lang=`), else the one chosen before
  (`localStorage`, `amico2000.lang`), else the first of the browser's
  languages that the page has, else English. A language that only came from
  the browser is not stored, so changing the browser's setting still counts.
- **Attributes cannot be doubled**, so a tooltip or a screen reader's label
  keeps its English and has the Italian beside it as `data-it-title`,
  `data-it-aria-label` or `data-it-content`; `lang.js` puts the right one in
  place and keeps the English as `data-en-...` to bring it back. `<title>` is
  done the same way with `data-it-text`.
- **What scripts say** is in `lang.js`, both languages together: the Run/Stop
  button, the NOR/SS switch's tooltip, the label of the displays, the tape's
  status line and the alerts after Load ROM or tape. `main.js` asks for them
  with `AmicoLang.t()`. The outcome of the last LOAD or SAVE is kept as a
  function, not a string, so it is said in the language the page is in when
  it is read. Each outcome still goes to the console in English: the console
  is not translated.
- **Changing language reloads nothing.** A reload would clear the machine's
  memory. If the address names a language it is rewritten, so that a reload
  does not bring the old one back.
- **The link** is one word in the page's top right corner, in the language it
  leads to. In the handheld layout, where the title leaves no room for a word,
  it is `IT` or `EN`, from the button's `data-code`. It is positioned against
  the page and takes no room in either layout.
- **The Italian** follows the same vocabulary as the guide, from the manual,
  and its labels are the ones the Italian guide quotes; a test holds the two
  together. The controls under the board are "Comandi dell'emulatore", which
  says what the English "Bench controls" leaves to the picture: these are the
  emulator's, not the board's.

Not translated, on purpose: the masthead, which is the machine's name; the
legends on the board and the keys; register names; and the console.

Two things had to be put right along the way, both found by the checks below.
The CPU line's Italian word, "in esecuzione", ran into the program counter
beside it, so in Italian that line has two columns. And the English page had
grown 13 px: the last paragraph of About this board was no longer the last
child, its Italian twin being after it, so it kept a margin it used to lose.

Known limits. A search engine and a link preview read the English that the
page is written in, since one address serves both languages; the Italian
guide has its own address. The reasons the machine and the tape codec give
for refusing something are English text made in `amico2000.js` and
`tape-audio.js`; they are shown as they are until the next commit.

## The Machine's Refusals in Italian

### Purpose and scope

The third commit for the site in Italian. After the second, an Italian reader
who loaded a damaged tape image was told "Errore nel caricamento del file:
Tape image is too short", and a refused SAVE read "Non registrato: the end
address is before the start address". The second half of each is made by
`amico2000.js` or `tape-audio.js`, in an `Error`'s message or in the
`problems` and `note` of a decoded recording.

Those two files are not given a language. They know nothing about the page,
which is the arrangement `AGENTS.md` describes, and their tests pin the
English words. So the Italian is in `lang.js`, as `MESSAGES_IT`: for each
message a pattern that matches it whole and a function from the match to the
same thing in Italian, carrying over the numbers, addresses and times.
`AmicoLang.message()` finds it; a message it does not know is shown as it is.
`main.js` passes through it the reason on the tape's status line, an error's
message in the load alert, and the decoder's problems and notes in the
summary of a recording. The console still gets each of them in English.

A table of patterns can fall behind the messages it matches. The check for
that is to make every message for real, by doing the thing that is refused:
a ROM image too large, a program over a ROM, five kinds of bad tape image,
four kinds of bad WAV file, a SAVE of a range that ends before it starts, a
LOAD of a program that is not there, a recording that breaks off, one with a
wrong checksum, and so on: 32 messages, of the 27 kinds the table has. Each
has to come back changed in Italian and unchanged in English, with every
number and address of the English still in it.

Not translated: `No matching tape record is available`, which only the
console commands `debug.saveTape()` and `debug.saveTapeAudio()` can raise,
and the two messages of `encodeWav()`, likewise. They go to the console.
